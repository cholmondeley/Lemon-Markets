* Screening skill is a validity correlation: how strongly your assessment of a candidate tracks their real performance. Hiring research puts it at about 0.18 for unstructured interviews and 0.44 for structured interviews (Sackett et al., 2022, Journal of Applied Psychology, Table 2, validity corrected for criterion unreliability; the paper's headline estimates, which also correct for range restriction, are 0.19 and 0.42). These are hiring benchmarks; there is no equivalent measured figure for dating, so on the Dating tab treat the scale as illustrative. The model shrinks the claim-versus-reality gap by a factor of √(1 − r²), the share of the spread your assessment can't explain, so even good screening leaves most of the gap in place.

How this actually works

The setup

Everyone has an actual quality (how good they really are) and an advertised quality (how good they look on the first pass — a résumé, a dating profile). People get discovered at a rate based on how they look. They get found out — and sent back onto the market — at a rate based on who they actually are.

Why liars pile up

An honest, high-quality match gets taken and stays taken — they leave the pool for good. Someone overselling gets taken too, but gets found out and bounced back quickly. Run that forward, and the people who keep reappearing in the pool are disproportionately the ones who oversold. The pool doesn't lie about individuals — it lies about the average.

What actually helps

Move fast on someone before the market has had time to sort them (reset the sim and look at t=0). Poach people who aren't even looking — in a fast-churning market, the ones already "taken" are the real high performers (try the "already taken" view). Or just get better at screening — narrowing the gap between claims and reality has the same effect as raising your bar.
