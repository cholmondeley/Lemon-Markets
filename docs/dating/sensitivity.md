# Dating models: calibration and sensitivities

Generated by `node scripts/dating/sensitivity.mjs`. Inputs: `src/data/dating/*.json` (digitized charts, the
Dating Calculator parquet, GSS). Every number below is recomputed on each run.

## 1. Attention market

Viewers agree on attractiveness with correlation ρ and each likes a fixed share of what she is shown. ρ is
fitted to the app founder's received-ratio histograms (100 views per profile; like rates weighted by
activity with exponent 0.14 for women and 0.30 for men so their means match the received means).

| Side | Fitted consensus ρ | KS distance | Median received (model / data) |
|---|---|---|---|
| Women rating men | 0.48 | 0.052 | 5.1% / 4.5% |
| Men rating women | 0.56 | 0.025 | 37.5% / 38.5% |

Men agree more about women than women do about men. The concentration on men comes from how few
profiles women like, not from stronger agreement (see the counterfactuals below).

### Share of all likes

Equal exposure is the like-ratio view (every profile shown equally). The Hinge rows add an exposure tilt κ
(views ∝ e^{κz}), fitted to Hinge's top-5% share alone: κ = 0.74 for men (a top-5% man is shown
4.2× as often as the median man), 0.86 for women. The other Hinge columns are out of sample.

| Who | Top 1% | Top 5% | Top 10% | Top 20% | Bottom 50% | Gini | Hinge (1/5/10/bottom 50) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Men, equal exposure | 5% | 18% | 31% | 49% | 18.9% | 0.45 |  |
| Men, Hinge exposure | 17% | 41% | 57% | 74% | 5.9% | 0.71 | 16 / 41 / 58 / 4 |
| Women, equal exposure | 2% | 10% | 18% | 33% | 32.7% | 0.24 |  |
| Women, Hinge exposure | 11% | 31% | 46% | 64% | 10.5% | 0.61 | 11 / 31 / 46 / 8 |

Offline cross-check (GSS 2000+, lifetime partners since 18, ages 25-45): the top 5% of men account for
40% of partners reported by men and the top 10% for 54% (women: 33% and 46%).

## 3. Commitment filter

Men differ only in intent: serious men pair off at rate λ, casual men at k_c·λ; relationships end at δ.
λ is proportional to the likes a man gets (equal exposure, the founder's app). b is the casual share of all men.
Target: the founder's chart, casual share on the app by attractiveness (8 groups): 23 22 30 33 35 41 46 52.

| k_c | b | x (median man) | x (top group) | Model casual share, least to most attractive | RMSE (pts) | Top group single |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 0 | 20% | 0.91 | 3.5 | 23 25 28 31 34 38 43 52 | 2.3 | 38% |
| 0 | 23% | 0.71 | 2.8 | 25 28 30 32 35 39 43 52 | 2.5 | 44% |
| 0.1 | 20% | 1.65 | 6.4 | 24 28 31 35 38 42 46 52 | 2.5 | 24% |
| 0.1 | 23% | 1.14 | 4.5 | 26 29 32 35 38 41 46 52 | 3.2 | 31% |
| 0.2 | 20% | 7.20 | 28.0 | 31 38 42 44 47 48 50 52 | 9.9 | 6% |
| 0.2 | 23% | 2.72 | 10.6 | 29 34 37 40 43 46 49 52 | 6.9 | 15% |

No intent difference by looks is needed: selection alone reproduces the gradient. It requires casual men to pair
off at under 23% of the serious rate when b = 20% (at any k_c above b(1−t)/((1−b)t) the top share can never reach
52%: with k_c = 0.3 the ceiling is 45%). x is how many times faster a serious man pairs off than a relationship ends.
